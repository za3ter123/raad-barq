1 USING AI RESPONSIBLY 
1.1 
The Core Message  
Artificial Intelligence is changing the way we design, learn, and compete, and in STEM Racing, it’s 
part of the future we’re building together. AI can help you think faster, design smarter, and tell your 
story more powerfully than ever before. But with great technology comes great responsibility. 
 
YOU CAN USE EVERY TOOL — BUT YOU MUST UNDERSTAND EVERY OUTCOME. 
AI is an incredible partner in creativity, research, and engineering, yet it’s only as powerful as the 
mind behind it. You are encouraged to explore every technology available to you, but remember: if 
you can’t explain how something works, you can’t claim it as your own. Understanding is 
ownership. 
 
BE TRANSPARENT. ALWAYS TELL JUDGES HOW AI WAS USED. 
AI should enhance your ideas, not replace them. Whenever you use AI in your project, whether for 
concept sketches, data summaries, or presentation design, include a short note or appendix in 
your portfolio to show honesty and accountability.

2 Introduction 
Innovation sits at the heart of STEM Racing™. From the first sketch of a car body to the final race 
down the 20-metre track, teams are encouraged to think creatively, use new technologies, and 
engineer bold solutions. 
Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools now offer new ways to design, research, and present ideas. Used 
responsibly, they can enhance learning and efficiency. Misused, they can undermine originality and 
the spirit of fair competition. 
This guide explains how teams may use, reference, and disclose AI tools ethically in all areas of 
the STEM Racing Challenge. It draws on global academic integrity standards and official STEM 
Racing Regulations to help every team innovate confidently while protecting their credibility and 
compliance. 
2.1 
Understanding AI in the STEM Racing Context 
Definitions: 
• 
Artificial Intelligence (AI) – a system capable of performing tasks that normally require 
human intelligence, such as pattern recognition, decision-making, or natural-language 
processing. 
• 
Generative AI – an AI model (for example, GPT-based chatbots or image generators) that 
creates new text, images, code, or data based on patterns in existing information. 
• 
Machine Learning – algorithms that allow systems to learn from data and improve 
automatically through experience. 
• 
Natural Language Processing (NLP) – the technology enabling AI systems to understand 
and generate human language.

3 Core Principles for Responsible AI Use 
3.1 
Integrity and Originality 
All work presented at the STEM Racing World Finals must represent the team’s own ideas, designs, 
and understanding. AI may assist, but the final creative and technical decisions must belong to the 
team. This aligns with:  
• Competition Regulations C2.18 – Plagiarism (work must be original and credited appropriately).  
• Technical Regulations T2.10 – Originality of Work (all CAD, CAM data, and text must be 
produced by the team).  
3.2 Transparency 
Teams must be open about any use of AI in written or visual work. A simple acknowledgement (see 
Section 4) ensures honesty and helps judges understand how technology was integrated. 
3.3 Verification and Human Oversight 
AI-generated outputs may contain factual errors, embedded bias, fabricated references, or 
technically plausible but incorrect claims. Teams remain fully responsible for verifying all AI-
assisted content before submission. Verification requirements differ depending on the project 
area. 
For Design & Engineering, technical claims must be supported by credible engineering sources 
such as peer-reviewed journals, recognised aerospace or physics textbooks, manufacturer 
datasheets, official software outputs, or validated testing data. Performance claims should be 
supported by real calculations, simulations, or physical testing where applicable. 
For Materials and Manufacturing, teams should reference manufacturer datasheets, material 
property databases, or verified engineering standards. 
For Project Management, timelines, risk analysis methods, and budgeting frameworks should be 
cross-checked against recognised methodologies or documented project practices. 
For Marketing & Enterprise, market statistics, demographic data, and financial projections must 
be supported by credible business reports, official data sources, or clearly cited research. 
For Performance Benchmarks, teams should reference official competition records, published 
STEM Racing data, or verified test results. 
Teams must not rely solely on AI-generated citations or statistics without independently confirming 
their authenticity. 
AI systems may generate inaccurate, fabricated, or unverifiable information, including: 
• 
Fabricated academic references 
• 
Invented statistics 
• 
Incorrect technical explanations 
• 
Misattributed quotes or data 
• 
Plausible but inaccurate scientific reasoning 
Such errors remain the responsibility of the team. 
Verification Checklist 
For every significant AI-assisted output, teams should complete the following process:

1. Identify the original source referenced (if any). 
2. Assess the credibility of the source. 
3. Cross-reference the claim against at least one independent source where appropriate. 
4. Confirm alignment with competition regulations and technical constraints. 
5. Record verification steps in the AI Assistance and Verification Log (see Appendix). 
Failure to conduct appropriate verification may result in a Competition Integrity Breach under 
Section 7. 
3.4 Accountability 
Each team member remains responsible for the content submitted. 
Judges may ask teams to explain how AI tools were used and verified. Failure to provide clear 
answers could affect scoring.

4 Referencing and Acknowledging AI Use 
4.1 Why Reference AI? 
Referencing AI sources follows international academic practice and prevents plagiarism. It 
demonstrates transparency, allows others to reproduce your process, and upholds the “Spirit of 
the Competition”. 
4.2 How to Reference AI Tools 
When an AI tool contributes text, imagery, or data, include an in-text acknowledgement and a full 
reference in your portfolio or appendix. 
Example 1 – Text Assistance (Harvard Style) 
Text generated using ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2026) in response to the prompt “Explain aerodynamic 
drag in model racing cars.” Reviewed and edited by Team Velocity. 
Reference List Entry: 
OpenAI (2026) ChatGPT [large language model]. Version 5. Available at: 
https://chat.openai.com (Accessed 10 Jan 2026). 
 
 
Example 2 – Image Generation (APA 7th Style) 
Image produced with DALL·E (OpenAI, 2026) based on the prompt “Team logo concept 
showing carbon fibre texture and blue aerodynamic flow lines.” Modified in Adobe Illustrator by 
the team. 
 
 
Example 3 – Research Summarisation 
Information retrieved from Consensus (2026) AI research platform. All cited papers were 
reviewed manually to confirm accuracy. 
 
 
4.3 Where to Place AI Acknowledgements 
All AI use must be clearly documented and attached to the team’s portfolio submission. 
AI acknowledgements may appear within the main body of the portfolio (e.g., footnotes or 
parenthetical references) and in presentation materials (e.g., slide footnotes or verbal 
disclaimers). However, in addition to any inline acknowledgements, all teams are required to 
complete and attach the official STEM Racing™ AI Assistance and Verification Log as an appendix 
to their portfolio (see Appendix: AI Assistance and Verification Log Template). 
The AI Assistance and Verification Log must document each instance of AI use within the project 
and include the following mandatory fields: date of use, AI tool and version, project area, prompt or 
task description, description of how the output was used and modified, verification steps 
undertaken, and the responsible team member. 
The completed log must be submitted as part of the portfolio for judging. Failure to include the log 
where AI tools have been used may be treated as a transparency breach under Section 7. The 
official template provided by STEM Racing™ must be used without modification, except for the 
addition of rows where necessary (see Appendix: AI Assistance and Verification Log Template).

5 Ethical Limits and Examples of Acceptable Use 
The use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools in STEM Racing projects is encouraged only when it 
enhances learning, creativity, and efficiency—not when it replaces the team’s own work.  
 
All project elements submitted for judging must be wholly created and understood by the team, as 
outlined in the official STEM Racing World Finals Regulations. Teams must also uphold the Spirit of 
the Competition, which values honesty, originality, and fair collaboration. 
 
AI can be a valuable aid for research, idea generation, or improving clarity, but teams must always 
ensure that final designs, portfolios, presentations, and data reflect their own reasoning and 
technical decisions. Below is a summary of what constitutes acceptable and unacceptable use

6 The Core Principle: Understanding What You Produce 
Artificial Intelligence can be a powerful assistant; it can suggest ideas, speed up research, or help 
visualise designs. However, in STEM Racing™ competitions, the real measure of success is 
understanding. 
You are welcome to use any AI or digital tool available, provided that: 
1. You know what the tool is doing. 
2. You understand why it produces certain results. 
3. You can explain those results clearly to the judges. 
When a judge asks, “Why did your AI suggest this shape?” or “How did this image represent your 
brand values?” The team must answer confidently and accurately. 
 
Relying on AI without comprehension shows a lack of learning, which contradicts the spirit of the 
competition. 
IN STEM RACING, TECHNOLOGY IS AN ASSISTANT — NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR 
UNDERSTANDING.

7 Academic Integrity and Judging Compliance 
Artificial Intelligence use is compatible with STEM Racing’s educational mission only when 
students remain the original authors, decision-makers, and accountable owners of their work. AI 
may support student development, but it must not replace student understanding, authorship, or 
accountability. 
7.1 
Judging Assessment Criteria 
Across all judging categories, Design & Engineering, Project Management, Enterprise, and 
Verbal Presentation, judges are instructed to assess three core principles: understanding, 
transparency, and integrity. 
With respect to understanding, judges must determine whether team members can clearly 
explain how AI tools were used in the development of their work, whether they can justify why 
specific outputs, designs, or wording were selected, and whether they demonstrate genuine 
technical and conceptual ownership of any AI-assisted outcomes. 
In assessing transparency, judges must evaluate whether AI usage has been clearly disclosed 
within the team’s portfolio, appendix, or presentation, and whether the scope and extent of AI 
involvement have been accurately and honestly described. 
In relation to integrity, judges must consider whether the submitted work reflects authentic 
student decision-making, whether technical claims, simulations, data, and designs are verifiable, 
and whether the team has avoided plagiarism, fabricated references, or unverifiable outputs. 
Material or deliberate failure to meet these standards may constitute a Competition Integrity 
Breach. 
7.2 Authenticity, Transparency, and Demonstrated Understanding 
7.2.1 
Principle 
The STEM Racing World Finals assesses student understanding, application, and ownership of 
work. The use of AI tools is permitted unless otherwise restricted, provided that all submitted 
work reflects genuine student understanding and authorship. 
Judges shall assess teams based on demonstrated understanding using the official scorecards. 
AI use in itself shall not result in a penalty. 
7.2.2 
Assessment Through Existing Scorecards 
Judges will evaluate all submitted work through the published judging criteria. 
Where a team is unable to clearly explain, justify, or demonstrate understanding of submitted 
work, this shall be reflected in the score awarded within the relevant judging category. No 
additional penalty shall be applied where reduced marks appropriately reflect the level of 
demonstrated understanding. 
7.2.3 
Mandatory Disclosure Requirement 
Where AI tools have been used in the development of any submitted work, teams must complete 
and attach the official STEM Racing™ AI Assistance and Verification Log as part of their portfolio 
submission (see Appendix). 
The AI Assistance Log is a mandatory submission component. Where AI has been used but no log 
is submitted, the judging panel shall notify the team and provide a reasonable opportunity to 
complete the log prior to final scoring.

If a completed log is not provided following notification, scores in the affected judging categories 
may be capped at a maximum of 80% of available marks to reflect the incomplete nature of the 
submission. This adjustment applies only to categories where undisclosed AI use is identified. 
HONEST DISCLOSURE OF AI USE WILL NOT NEGATIVELY AFFECT A TEAM’S SCORE. 
7.2.4 
Integrity Review (Exceptional Circumstances) 
Separate integrity action shall only be considered where there is clear evidence of: 
• 
Deliberate misrepresentation of authorship 
• 
Fabricated technical data, simulations, or results 
• 
Plagiarism (including AI-assisted concealment) 
• 
Submission of work without demonstrable student understanding or involvement 
In such cases, the team may receive a score of zero in the affected category. 
Any decision under this clause must be reviewed and confirmed by the Chair of Judges. The ICC 
and supervising teacher will be formally notified. Teams will always be given the opportunity to 
explain their work before any integrity decision is made. 
7.2.5 
Proportionality 
Judges must distinguish between: 
• 
A quality or depth issue (to be reflected in normal scoring under 7.2.2), 
• 
A disclosure omission (to be addressed through the notification process under 7.2.3), 
and 
• 
An integrity or authenticity breach (to be reviewed under 7.2.4). 
Integrity sanctions are expected to be rare and applied only where evidence is clear and material. 
The purpose of this framework is to encourage responsible use of AI, not to discourage its 
adoption. 
7.3  Escalation and Decision Process 
If judges identify a concern regarding AI use or academic integrity, the issue will be reviewed by the 
judging panel and referred to the Chair of Judges, where required. 
Teams will always be given the opportunity to explain their work and demonstrate understanding of 
how AI was used. 
After considering the team’s explanation, a decision will be made in accordance with this Article 
and formally recorded.
